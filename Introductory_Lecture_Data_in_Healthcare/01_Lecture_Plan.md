# Instructor plan — 75 minutes

## Teaching arc

**I recognize this problem → I can ask a better question → I can reason with a small table → I see the wider system → I can imagine a useful contribution → I know my next step.**

**Unifying purpose: reduce uncertainty (تقليل عدم اليقين) to support better decisions.** Introduce this at the opening, demonstrate it in the table activity, organize the four types around it, and return to it at the close. Analysis can also reveal that we know less than we thought; that is useful if it prevents an unjustified decision. Do not promise a fixed decrease in uncertainty at every step.

Use one recurring fictional story: a person arrives to collect a regularly needed medicine and it is unavailable. No disease-specific treatment discussion is needed. A doctor sees continuity of care; a pharmacist sees availability; a nurse sees follow-up; someone outside healthcare sees a supply and access problem.

## Outcomes

Participants should be able to:

1. Explain data analysis in everyday language.
2. Read what a row and column represent in a small table.
3. Compare a count with a proportion using a supplied example.
4. Separate evidence, hypothesis, and a proposed action.
5. Connect individual care to operations, population health, and wider systems.
6. Identify a possible project or work direction and one realistic first step.
7. Match the four analytics types to their questions and explain why reducing uncertainty does not mean eliminating it.

## 00–05 — Opening and first impression

Spend about 20 seconds acknowledging the postponed session, then move to the new beginning:

> المرة الفاتت واجهتنا مشكلة تقنية وما قدرنا نبدأ. الفترة دي أتاحت لناس جدد ينضموا لينا، فمرحب بيكم جميعاً. محاضرة اليوم بداية مشتركة؛ ما محتاج تكون حضرت المحاضرات السابقة.

Introduce yourself for 45–60 seconds using only true details:

> أنا [الاسم]، وخلفيتي في [وصف مختصر ودقيق]. اهتمامي بتعليم تحليل البيانات بدأ من [تجربة حقيقية في جملة]. هدفي في الدورة إنك تتعلم تحول سؤال من واقعك إلى تحليل تقدر تشرحه وتراجعه.

Then start the story:

> تخيل زول وصل عشان يستلم دواء محتاجه بصورة منتظمة، ولقاه غير متوفر. هل المشكلة في الطلب؟ في التوريد؟ في التوزيع؟ ولا في التسجيل؟ قبل ما نختار الحل، محتاجين نعرف شنو؟

Name the purpose in everyday language:

> عدم اليقين يعني في حاجات لسه ما عارفنها: حجم المشكلة شنو؟ سببها شنو؟ وممكن نعمل شنو؟ تحليل البيانات بيساعدنا نقلل عدم اليقين، ونوضح الحاجات اللسه ما عارفنها، عشان القرار يكون مبني على دليل أوضح. ما معناه حنصل ليقين كامل.

Invite one short chat answer: “مشكلة بتتكرر في شغلك أو مجتمعك وتتمنى تفهمها بالأرقام؟” Read two examples, including a nonclinical one when available. Do not let this become an open microphone round.

The opening should demonstrate a welcoming, clear, evidence-aware teaching style. Long biographies and promises about employment do not help establish it.

## 05–12 — A bigger picture

Expand the same drawing:

- **Person:** Can they obtain what they need, at a cost and distance they can manage?
- **Facility:** Which items are unavailable, how often, and for how long?
- **Community:** Who cannot reach the facility and never appears in its records?
- **Connected systems:** What do purchasing, budgets, transport, information systems, and policy change?

Ask: “لو وظيفتك في جزء واحد من السلسلة، هل معناها إن تأثيرك لازم يقيف هناك؟” Pause, then invite one answer.

Make room for the other 20%: medicine supply and food supply both involve demand, stock, delivery, and waste. Different domains require different context, but many analytical questions transfer.

## 12–20 — A minimum foundation

Use the table in `03_Examples_and_Opportunities.md`, initially showing only its first two numeric columns. Define words while pointing at the actual objects:

> البيانات هنا ملاحظات سجلناها. الـ Dataset هو الجدول المجمع. الصف الواحد بيمثل منشأة واحدة خلال شهر واحد. العمود بيمثل حاجة سجلناها عن المنشأة.

Clarify that this is already a summary table; one row is not a patient. A different table might use one row per collection attempt. The question determines the suitable unit of observation.

Describe analysis as: “بنرتب ونقارن ونراجع الملاحظات عشان نجاوب سؤال ونساعد في قرار.” Ask someone to read the first row in their own words.

Draw the workflow:

**Question → Collect → Check → Compare → Explain → Act → Review**

Introduce only the terms needed now. Leave the longer glossary as instructor support.

## 20–32 — A small analytical win

Follow the complete exercise and answer key in `03_Examples_and_Opportunities.md`.

Pacing: two minutes to predict; three to reveal denominators and compare; three to discuss alternative explanations; four to formulate a recommendation and what to check next.

The discovery is that Facility A has more unsuccessful attempts, while Facility B has the greater percentage. Different questions lead to different priorities. This is a tradeoff to explain, not a trick with one universally correct winner.

Say: “إنتو هسع عملتوا تحليل: حددتوا السؤال، قارنتوا، وانتبهتوا لمعلومة كانت ناقصة. الخطوة الجاية نتعلم نعمل ده بصورة منظمة وقابلة للمراجعة.”

## 32–40 — Technical vocabulary as four useful questions

Use the four types named in KMS's “Different Types of Data Analytics in Healthcare” section (Source S4). Teach them in the order below so the same story moves from describing events to investigating explanations, estimating the future, and comparing actions. This is a teaching order, not a compulsory sequence for every analysis.

| Plain question | English term | Same-story example | What it does not establish |
|---|---|---|---|
| What happened? | Descriptive analytics | What share of recorded attempts failed? | Why they failed. |
| What might explain it? | Diagnostic analytics | Do failures coincide with late deliveries or recording changes? | That an observed relationship proves a cause. |
| What might happen? | Predictive analytics | Under stated assumptions, what might next month's demand be? | A guaranteed future. |
| What action best fits our goal and constraints? | Prescriptive analytics | Compare feasible ordering or distribution options under budget, delivery, and safety constraints. | Automatic authority to act. |

Diagnostic here means investigating explanations; it does not mean diagnosing a patient. The four categories organize questions; they are not a ladder everyone must climb in every project.

Connect the four cards to one heading: **Reduce uncertainty / تقليل عدم اليقين**.

| Type | Uncertainty it helps address in our story | What remains uncertain |
|---|---|---|
| Descriptive — وصفي | How large is the recorded availability problem, and where? | Unrecorded attempts and people who never reached a facility. |
| Diagnostic — تشخيصي / البحث عن التفسير | Which explanations are supported by stock, delivery, and recording evidence? | Competing causes; a relationship alone does not establish causation. |
| Predictive — تنبؤي | What demand or shortage might occur next month under stated assumptions? | Forecast error and changes in conditions. |
| Prescriptive — توجيهي / اقتراح الإجراء | Which feasible action best fits a stated goal, budget, and constraints? | Whether assumptions hold and how well the action will work in practice. |

Speaking cue:

> حصل شنو؟ ممكن يكون حصل ليه؟ متوقع يحصل شنو؟ وبناءً على هدفنا وظروفنا، نعمل شنو؟ كل نوع بيساعدنا نجاوب سؤال مختلف ونقلل جزء من عدم اليقين. وفي كل مرة بنقول بوضوح شنو الدليل وشنو اللسه ما معروف.

Keep this within the existing eight minutes: use the first table for the questions and examples, then the second as speaker support for explaining the uncertainty. The board contains only four concise cards, not two text-heavy tables.

Give AI at most one minute if it comes up: “الـ AI ممكن يساعد في بعض المهام، لكنه محتاج سؤال واضح وبيانات مناسبة ومراجعة. جدول صغير وتحليل بسيط ممكن يساعدونا من البداية.” Do not expand into model families or clinical AI performance claims.

## 40–49 — Local relevance and wider impact

**Sudan, about three minutes:** WHO's report covering March–April 2025 documents early warning reporting in Sudan and incomplete or delayed submissions. Use it as a dated example, not a statement about today's coverage. Draw reporting site → report → review → investigation → response. Ask: “لو التقرير ما وصل، هل نكتب صفر حالات؟” Expected answer: no; missing reports and zero events mean different things. See Sources S6–S7.

**Global, about three minutes:** WHO's Health Inequality Monitor makes data available by population groups. Show a conceptual split of one overall result by place of residence or another relevant group. Label any invented diagram values as synthetic. Ask: “ممكن الرقم العام يكون كويس وفي مجموعة ما وصلتها الخدمة؟” The point is whose experience disappears in an overall figure. See S8.

**Transfer, about three minutes:** Participants choose a problem: missed appointments, stock expiry, delayed deliveries, water-service access, research follow-up, or school attendance. Ask for the decision, the people affected, and one missing piece of information. Take two responses. This preserves both Sudanese relevance and applicability elsewhere.

## 49–58 — Opportunities beyond the immediate workplace

Use the detailed map in `03_Examples_and_Opportunities.md`. Talk through three contrasting routes: humanitarian reporting, supply-chain analysis, and research support. Each needs a question, an example output, and an honest account of additional skills.

Then briefly show digital health, quality improvement, policy, and work in other sectors. Job labels are illustrative search terms, not a list of current vacancies or promises of entry eligibility.

> معرفتك بالمجال بتساعدك تسأل أسئلة مهمة وتلاحظ أخطاء ممكن غيرك ما ينتبه ليها. ومع مهارات التحليل، ممكن تساهم في فرق البحث والتشغيل والمنظمات والمنتجات الرقمية. كل مسار عنده متطلبات إضافية، والدورة بتديك بداية عملية تبني عليها.

Three motivations to connect to their own examples: understand a frustrating recurring problem; make an argument others can inspect; develop work samples that show what they can do. Skills can strengthen a current role, support research, or help explore a new direction.

## 58–65 — Make the course feel reachable

Draw the learning route as outputs rather than a wall of software logos:

| Skill | Contribution to the story |
|---|---|
| Spreadsheets | Organize stock records and check totals and missing values. |
| SQL | Retrieve and combine records stored in separate tables. |
| Visualization / Tableau | Make differences across facilities and time easy to see. |
| Applied statistics | Judge variation, comparisons, and the strength of an explanation. |
| Python, if retained in the final syllabus | Repeat a checked analysis when new records arrive. |
| Project and communication | Document a question, methods, limitations, findings, and a next step. |

Point out the published spreadsheet module's foundational path: understanding tables comes before cleaning, summaries, charts, and recommendations. Newcomers start there. Previous attendees deepen their reasoning in the activity instead of sitting through a recap of six lectures.

Two-minute independent task, in chat or on paper:

> اختار مشكلة واحدة، واكتب: سؤال واضح + معلومتين محتاجهم + شكل بسيط للنتيجة.

Offer an example if needed: “أي صنف يتكرر عدم توفره؟ أحتاج اسم الصنف وتواريخ عدم التوفر. الناتج جدول مقارنة مع ملاحظات عن السجلات الناقصة.” For later practice use supplied synthetic records or suitable public data.

## 65–75 — Discussion and closing

Reserve seven minutes for questions. If nobody asks, use: “شنو الفرق بين رقم بيوصف المشكلة ورقم بيثبت سببها؟” or “أي مسار حسيت إنه قريب من اهتماماتك؟”

Exit ticket, three minutes including closing:

1. “أنا مهتم أفهم ___، ومحتاج بيانات عن ___، وأول نتيجة ممكن أعملها ___.”
2. Explain why “B has a higher recorded failure percentage” is evidence, while “B has worse management” is an unsupported conclusion.

> ما مطلوب منك تطلع الليلة حافظ مصطلحات كثيرة. المطلوب تطلع شايف سؤال يستاهل تفهمه، وعارف إنك تقدر تبدأ بخطوة صغيرة. في الدورة حنتعلم نجمع وننظم ونحلل ونشرح، ونتأكد إن استنتاجنا قدر الدليل الموجود.

End on: “هدفنا نقلل عدم اليقين عشان نختار خطوة على أساس أوضح، ونفضل عارفين حدود الدليل.”

## Delivery safeguards

- Use Arabic explanations before naming English terms. Pause after every new concept.
- Keep questions visible and accept chat or spoken responses. Silence gets a worked example, not an assumption that everyone understood.
- In the case, explicitly ask who is missing from the records. Facility visitors are not the whole community.
- Use synthetic examples; do not invite participants to share identifiable patient records. Removing names alone is not a complete privacy safeguard.
- Keep observed data, possible causes, recommendations, and actual outcomes distinct. A recommendation creates value only if feasible action and subsequent review follow.
- Keep a local copy of the eventual board and static scene exports. Test screen sharing, Arabic labels, zoom, and chat before class. Continue from the numbered scene notes if the whiteboard fails.
