# Data in Healthcare — From Everyday Problems to Better Decisions

**Arabic title:** البيانات في الرعاية الصحية: من مشكلة يومية إلى قرار أفضل  
**Format:** 75 minutes, including discussion; Arabic delivery with English technical terms.  
**Status:** English freeform board built with circular clusters, curved arrows, and handwritten labels.  
**Audience:** Mixed backgrounds; more than 80% from healthcare, according to the instructor. Around 70% missed the previous six lectures. No prior lecture, spreadsheet skill, or statistical knowledge assumed.

## The lecture's promise

By the end, a participant can take a familiar problem, ask a useful question, identify some needed data, and suggest a cautious next step. They can also name one direction in which these skills could expand their work.

The central message:

> تحليل البيانات بيساعدنا نقلل عدم اليقين (Reduce uncertainty)، ونوضح شنو عرفنا وشنو لسه محتاجين نعرفه، عشان نختار خطوة على أساس أوضح.

> خبرتك بتساعدك تلاحظ المشكلة. تحليل البيانات بيساعدك تفهم حجمها، وتقارن الخيارات، وتتابع هل القرار حسّن الوضع فعلاً.

The four types of analytics are a core teaching anchor: **Descriptive, Diagnostic, Predictive, and Prescriptive**. Scene 5 connects each to a different uncertainty in the same medicine-availability story. Appropriate analysis can reduce uncertainty and expose gaps; it does not guarantee certainty or automatically remove bias.

Healthcare is our entry point into a transferable way of thinking. The story expands from a patient, to a facility, to a community, to systems that connect healthcare with transport, procurement, water, finance, and public policy.

## What success looks like

At the end, learners complete: “I want to understand ___, I would need ___, and my first useful output would be ___.” They distinguish an observation from a possible explanation. Their motivation comes from recognizing a problem they could start investigating, supported by a believable learning route.

## Scope

This lecture introduces data thinking and its possibilities. Technical practice continues in the bootcamp. The spreadsheet, SQL, visualization, and statistics links follow the repo's existing module plans; the Python link comes from the initial draft and should be confirmed against the final syllabus.
